## What Is Actually Happening Inside Your Body During a Hangover?

A hangover is much more than dehydration. Your body is trying to recover from several different effects of alcohol at the same time. That's why drinking a bottle of water before bed rarely fixes the problem.

**What We Know**

Alcohol suppresses vasopressin (also called antidiuretic hormone, or ADH), the hormone that helps your kidneys conserve water. Without it, your kidneys excrete more water than normal, leaving you dehydrated by the time you wake up. According to the [National Institute on Alcohol Abuse and Alcoholism](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/brochures-and-fact-sheets/hangovers) (NIAAA), this contributes directly to many classic hangover symptoms, including headache, fatigue, dry mouth, and intense thirst.

But dehydration is only one piece of the puzzle.

Alcohol also disrupts your electrolytes, depletes certain B vitamins, irritates your stomach, alters blood sugar regulation, and produces acetaldehyde, which is a toxic byproduct created as your liver breaks down alcohol.

**Why This Matters**

Think of a hangover as five different problems happening at once:

- **Headache & thirst** → Primarily dehydration and fluid loss.
- **Fatigue & brain fog** → Poor sleep, dehydration, and depletion of nutrients involved in normal energy metabolism.
- **Muscle cramps & weakness** → Loss of fluids and electrolytes such as potassium and magnesium.
- **Nausea** → Stomach irritation and the effects of acetaldehyde.
- **Shakiness** → Blood sugar fluctuations and ongoing dehydration.

Once you understand what's driving your symptoms, the next question becomes obvious: Can IV therapy help address them?

> Alcohol suppresses vasopressin (ADH), causing the kidneys to excrete far more fluid than normal. The NIAAA identifies this mechanism as the direct driver of hangover dehydration, headache, thirst, and fatigue ([NIAAA](https://www.niaaa.nih.gov/publications/brochures-and-fact-sheets/hangovers)).

## Why Does Cabo Make a Hangover So Much Worse?

A hangover in Cabo isn't the same as a hangover back home.

By the time many visitors order their first margarita, they've already spent hours in the sun, at the pool, beach club, on the golf course, or walking the marina. Add alcohol to an already dehydrated body, and the effects compound quickly.

**What We Know**

Los Cabos sits within the Sonoran Desert, where high temperatures, intense UV exposure, and sweating all increase fluid & electrolyte loss before alcohol's diuretic effects even begin.

Alcohol then suppresses vasopressin (ADH), causing your kidneys to excrete even more water. The result is a much larger hydration deficit than alcohol alone.

**Why This Matters**

Imagine two people drinking the same four cocktails. One spends the afternoon in an air-conditioned restaurant. The other spends it under the Cabo sun. By the next morning, the second person isn't just recovering from alcohol, they're recovering from alcohol and heat-related dehydration.

That's why hangovers in Cabo often feel so much worse than they do back home.

> Alcohol causes the body to expel approximately 800-1,000 ml of urine for every 250 ml of alcohol consumed, a roughly 4:1 fluid loss ratio ([Cleveland Clinic](https://health.clevelandclinic.org/does-alcohol-dehydrate-you), 2024). In Cabo's extreme heat and UV conditions, additional fluid loss from sweating compounds this depletion significantly.

## What Does IV Therapy Actually Help With?

IV therapy isn't a cure for a hangover but it works by addressing the biggest reasons people feel unwell after drinking.

**What It Can Help With**

A well-formulated hangover IV rapidly replaces fluids and electrolytes lost through alcohol, supports normal energy metabolism with B vitamins, and can include physician-approved medications to help relieve nausea when clinically appropriate. Antioxidants such as glutathione can help support the body's natural response to the oxidative stress associated with alcohol metabolism.

For guests who are dehydrated, nauseated, or struggling to tolerate food and fluids, delivering these therapies directly into the bloodstream can provide meaningful symptom relief and help them recover more comfortably.

**What It Can't Do**

No IV can instantly erase the effects of alcohol or eliminate the need for time, sleep, and proper nutrition. Your liver still has to metabolize alcohol and its byproducts (acetaldehyde). IV therapy works by supporting your natural processes of recovery.

**Clinical Perspective**

Most guests just want to feel better and simply don't want to lose an entire day of their vacation.

When dehydration, nausea, and electrolyte imbalances are addressed, most feel well enough to enjoy lunch, spend the afternoon by the pool, play a round of golf, or head out for dinner.

That's the goal: helping you get back to enjoying Cabo sooner!
